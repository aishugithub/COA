The subject

Why this subject

Computer Organization & Architecture is where the abstraction finally drops away. Students spend years writing code that "just runs"; here they get to see what's really happening beneath it — how instructions move through a datapath, how memory is organised, how the machine is built to be fast. When that click happens for a student, it's one of the most rewarding moments in teaching.

I care about this subject because it rewards curiosity about the invisible. Nothing here is magic once you look closely, and getting students to look closely is exactly the kind of teaching I love.

Teaching

How I teach it

I'm a passionate teacher, and I take real care to make every class interactive and engaging. The deck isn't a script to read out — it's the scaffolding for a live conversation with the room, full of questions, analogies and moments where we work something out together.

A note on the materials

These slides are the visible skeleton of the class. When I deliver them live, there's always something subtler and deeper in the talk than the deck alone can hold — the connections drawn in the moment, the intuition behind a diagram, the aside that makes an idea land. If you use these materials, treat the deck as a starting point and bring your own voice to bridge the gap.

Free to use. These materials are open for anyone — students, teachers, self-learners — to learn from or teach with. A mention is always appreciated, but not required.
